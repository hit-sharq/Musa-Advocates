# All You Need to Know About Debt Collection in Kenya

Debt recovery is essential for maintaining financial stability for businesses and individuals. Whether the debt arises from unpaid loans, commercial transactions, or contractual obligations, it significantly disrupts your cash flow. Navigating the recovery process in Kenya requires a clear understanding of the legal framework governing debt collection, which includes court procedures, enforcement mechanisms, and even alternative dispute resolution options.

## How Are Debt Collection Proceedings Commenced in Kenya?

Normally, the first step is issuing a **time-bound demand letter** to the debtor. This serves as a formal notice demanding performance or payment of the sum of money owed in honour of a contractual commitment within a definite period.

## What Is the Significance of a Demand Letter?

A formal demand serves as a **preventive measure** against incurring additional costs of suit should the claim be admitted. Issuing a demand letter further mitigates against filing vexatious or suits brought out of malice. Where a formal demand is ignored, then the next available option is institution of debt recovery proceedings against a debtor.

## What Are the Formal Forums for Filing Debt Recovery Proceedings?

This is greatly influenced by the amount claimed and/or owed. Accordingly:

1. **Small Claims Court** — Section 12(3) of the Small Claims Court Act limits jurisdiction to **One Million Kenyan Shillings**
2. **Magistrate Courts** — As per Section 7 of the Magistrates' Court Act, Magistrate Courts have jurisdiction over cases where the value does not exceed **Twenty Million Kenyan Shillings**
3. **High Court** — By virtue of Article 165(3) of the Constitution, the High Court is the appropriate forum for matters exceeding **Twenty Million Kenyan Shillings**

## Key Definitions

### Who Is a Judgment Debtor?
A judgment debtor is a person whom a judgment has been entered against by a Court of Law.

### Who Is a Decree Holder?
A Decree Holder is the person whom a judgment has been rendered in favour of in civil proceedings.

## What Is the Time Limitation for Filing Debt Recovery Proceedings?

Given that almost all debt recovery proceedings emanate from either Oral or Written Agreements, they ought to be filed reasonably within **Six (6) years**. This is in accordance with Section 4 of the Limitation of Actions Act.

## When Is the Time for Filing Extended?

The limitation of time may be extended by virtue of **Section 22, 26 & 27** of the Limitation of Actions Act. These sections allow extension of time as a result of:

1. Disability
2. Fraud
3. Mistake
4. Ignorance of Material Facts

## Remedies Against a Judgment Debtor

In circumstances where a decree holder pursues enforcement, the following options are available:

### i. Garnishee Proceedings
As per **Order 23 of the Civil Procedure Rules**, this enables a decree holder to reach and secure money due to the judgment debtor, which is held lawfully by a third party through attachment of debts. A case in point is attachment of a judgment debtor's money held by a bank in their account.

### ii. Court Enforcement
Section 38 of the Civil Procedure Act provides that the Court has power to enforce execution on application of a decree holder. The Court may order execution of a decree:

1. By delivery of any property specifically decreed
2. By attachment and sale, or by sale without attachment, of any property
3. By attachment of debts
4. By arrest and detention in prison of any person
5. By appointing a receiver
6. In such other manner as the nature of the relief granted may require

## What If a Debtor Tries to Delay or Obstruct the Court Process?

The most appropriate approach is to make an **interlocutory application** seeking arrest and attachment. This ensures the judgment debtor is called upon to furnish security for his appearance as provided under **Order 39 of the Civil Procedure Rules**. Importantly, the defendant cannot be arrested if he settles any sum specified in the warrant as sufficient to satisfy the plaintiff's claim. The amount paid is normally held in deposit by the Court until the suit is disposed of or until further Court order is issued.

## Can a Judgment Be Settled in Installments?

**Absolutely!** Order 21 Rule 12 of the Civil Procedure Rules allows for direct payment in installments where a Decree has been issued for payment of money.
